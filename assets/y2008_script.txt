2008 was a turning point for cinema. Six movies came out that year that still shape what we watch today. Here's every one of them, explained.

First: Iron Man. Robert Downey Jr. played a billionaire weapons dealer who builds a suit of armor to save himself — and accidentally launched the biggest franchise in movie history. The Marvel Cinematic Universe started right here.

Then came The Dark Knight. Heath Ledger's Joker wasn't just a villain — he was a force of chaos that redefined what a comic book movie could be. It won two Oscars, including Best Supporting Actor — a posthumous tribute to cinema's most iconic villain.

Pixar gave us WALL-E — a little robot left alone on an abandoned Earth, falling in love with the last plant alive. The first thirty minutes have almost no dialogue, and it's the most emotionally devastating thing Pixar ever made.

Spielberg took one last swing at Indiana Jones with Kingdom of the Crystal Skull. It wasn't the best received — but the adventure, the practical effects, and the chemistry between Harrison Ford and Cate Blanchett made it unforgettable.

DreamWorks hit us with Kung Fu Panda — a clumsy panda who becomes the Dragon Warrior. It introduced one of the most lovable characters in animation history and proved DreamWorks could hang with Pixar.

And finally — The Incredible Hulk. Most people forget this one, but it's the movie that actually started the MCU. That post-credits scene? That's where it all began. Without the Hulk, there's no Avengers.

2008 gave us the birth of the modern blockbuster — the year superhero movies became the dominant force in cinema. Every movie we love today traces back to this one year. This is 2008 — the year everything changed.
