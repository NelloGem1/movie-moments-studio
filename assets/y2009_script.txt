2009 was the year movies went back to big — literally. Avatar became the highest-grossing film ever made, and a new generation of blockbusters was born. Here are the movies that defined it.

First: Avatar. James Cameron waited twelve years to make this — a sci-fi epic about a paraplegic Marine who falls in love with an alien world. It looked impossible, cost half a billion dollars, and became the biggest movie of all time. Cinematic technology changed forever.

Then J.J. Abrams rebooted Star Trek for a new generation. Chris Pine and Zachary Quinto brought fresh energy to a fifty-year-old franchise — and proved that reboots could work if you respected the original.

Robert Downey Jr. returned as Sherlock Holmes — but this wasn't the deerstalker version. Guy Ritchie turned it into a slick action-adventure with boxing matches, explosions, and Downey at his absolute peak. Watson never stood a chance.

The Hangover gave us the ultimate buddy comedy — three friends wake up in Vegas with no memory and a tiger in the bathroom. It became the highest-grossing comedy of the decade and launched a franchise that still makes people laugh.

Pixar surprised everyone with Up — a widower ties thousands of balloons to his house and flies to South America. The first ten minutes are the saddest thing Pixar ever made, and the rest is pure adventure.

And finally, Roland Emmerich went full disaster — 2012. Volcanoes, earthquakes, the end of the world as we know it. It wasn't subtle, but it was exactly what audiences wanted after a tough year.

2009 proved that big movies still mattered — the year audiences voted with their wallets for spectacle, heart, and stories worth telling. This is 2009 — the year movies went bigger than ever.
